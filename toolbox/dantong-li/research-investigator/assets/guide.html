Skill guide · research-investigator

Give it a direction.
It keeps the research going.

In short. You give a research direction, your references (a ZIP is fine) and the one thing that differs from them, such as “now allow resource X”. The skill maps what is known, finds which known bounds survive the change and which break, and turns that into precise, vetted conjectures. It attacks each conjecture with a proof lane built on the Cogentic method: parallel provers, independent adversarial verifiers and a ledger of checked lemmas. Every result, failure and objection feeds new questions. It keeps working until you stop it, and it reports only what its checks support.

  • Two layers: frontier program + proof lanes
  • You never dispatch roles yourself
  • Every result carries an honest label
  • Stop any time: a message or a STOP file

1What you give it and what you get back

You make one request: a direction (a topic, an idea or a family of questions), the references, and the key difference from them. Limits are optional. You do not write conjectures or prompt agents one by one. You get a run folder that grows while the run works. Its front page, progress.md, starts with what is now known, in plain sentences. A picture shows the known bounds against the amount of X. Then come a before/after table of the frontier, results with their labels, refuted conjectures, and what is running now.

If you give one precise claim instead (“prove or disprove: …”), the skill runs in focused mode: one proof lane on your exact statement, opened at once as in the paper, while the literature check and the model run beside it. It has a disproof target from the start; weaker fallbacks are added at the next round. If the claim is false only under a literal reading you probably did not mean (a boundary case, or two definitions in a source that disagree), it tells you at once and then works on the natural reading. Once your claim is settled, it writes the manuscript (or the refutation report) at once and tells you. Then it keeps researching from that result until you stop it, as a full program. After a proof, it first asks for the converse for every method of the same kind, not only for the method in your claim. After a refutation, it asks where the claim still holds. Then come stronger versions and new proposals. If you want only this claim, say so, and it stops at the manuscript. If a source already settles the claim, it tells you at once. The proof of your claim is the use the Cogentic paper describes. A precise conjecture inside a broader request is only a first question.

2The two layers

The outer layer decides what is worth proving. The inner layer proves or refutes it. One orchestrator starts every task and routes every result, but holds no mathematical opinion.

Why two layers? Cogentic's orchestrator gets one precise problem per run (its Appendix A). “Now allow X” is not one; the outer layer turns it into such problems.

The orchestrator routes. It is the agent you talk to. It starts tasks, files their outputs and writes the run's state, reading only each worker's short result header. It never proves, never recommends a technique, and never calls a direction promising or dead (invariant I1 in SKILL.md). All state lives in the run folder, so a run survives interruptions (I7).

The outer layer chooses questions. Literature reviewers and scouts gather evidence; scout patterns are conjectures, never proofs. The investigator owns the agenda, including how many provers each lane gets. The skeptic attacks models, questions and headline claims for loopholes, triviality, known results and unfair comparisons.

The inner layer proves. Each open question gets its own proof lane, which runs rounds as in the paper (Figure 3); lanes never wait for each other. Inside a round, the orchestrator assigns directions to provers without opinion, as in the paper. v6 adds fixed allocation rules (A1–A11) so this stays mechanical.

Milestones. The investigator keeps up to three candidate headlines in frontier/story.md. A milestone comes when every claim of a headline is accepted, or when you ask. The skeptic checks the claim, a comparator picks the strongest results, a writer drafts a manuscript, and a PAPER verifier audits it. Lanes keep running.

3One proof-lane round

A round turns a lane statement into drafts, and drafts into accepted results, objections or reusable lemmas. What a round learns goes into three files that the next round starts from.

Each lane works on a ladder of targets. R1 is the strongest; each lower rung (R2, R3, …) is strictly weaker and implied by the one above. D is the disproof target. A result has fidelity MATCH when it proves the claimed rung or something stronger, in the same model.

  1. Plan. The orchestrator gives each prover one direction: PROVE a rung, DISPROVE (target D), SPECIAL-CASE, or REPAIR a near-complete draft. It says what to work on, never how, by fixed rules A1–A11. In round 1, about half the provers take the current rung. One takes D when the skeptic rates the conjecture mixed or doubtful, or when four or more provers run. With three or more provers, one takes the next weaker rung if a slot is left.
  2. Brief. Each prover gets its own summarizer, which reads all earlier attempts, verdicts and ledger entries and picks what matters. Independent summarizers give provers on the same direction different starting points.
  3. Prove. Provers work in parallel. Each returns a lemma DAG (lemmas plus what each depends on) or a precise obstacle.
  4. Freeze and verify. Each draft is frozen as a candidate (C014, …): copied and fingerprinted, so the reviewed text is the accepted text. One verifier reads it alone, line by line. Another reads all of the round's drafts side by side, looking for shared blind spots, and ranks which are nearly complete. A draft with a declared gap gets a SALVAGE review instead: its proved steps are checked so they can reach the ledger, but the draft is never accepted. A scout checks computable claims on small cases; a contradiction is a critical objection. If a repair draft disputes an objection, a fresh verifier rules on it (ADJUDICATE). Verifiers assume every step is wrong until justified and every citation wrong until opened and checked.
  5. Close. The record of attempts (each attempt, its method and the objection it failed on) is updated, and passing candidates are accepted. The ledger auditor turns steps that verifiers confirmed, even inside failed drafts, into stand-alone lemmas; each is re-checked without its parent proof before it enters the ledger. A step refuted by an explicit counterexample becomes a checked dead end. The process advisor reviews logs across the run and updates the standing instructions. The next round's briefings start only after these return, so the next round uses what this one learned.

Lane events. An accepted lower rung moves the lane up to the next stronger rung. An accepted top rung lets in-flight drafts finish (one may be stronger), then closes the lane. A refutation closes it too; both lead to successor questions. A stall (two rounds with no new accepted rung, ledger entry or kind of objection, or a report that drafts fail at the same step) holds the lane and triggers a targeted literature search of the stuck step. The paper re-dispatches literature reviewers on a stall; the two-round trigger is v6's. After its budget (eight rounds by default) a lane is held too, and the investigator continues, descends or parks it. One table in program.md lists every event, hold and release. Cost: a round with N provers takes about 3N + 1 model calls, plus a few for auditing and advice. One lane costs about the paper's per-problem budget: about 100 calls, up to about 1,000 for the hardest. A program multiplies this by the number of lanes.

4How a question is born and renewed

Questions come from evidence, not guesses: a known bound that breaks under the change, an unknown point on a curve, a small-case table. Each is written as an exact statement with fallback targets, and a skeptic attacks it before any prover sees it.

  1. Sources. The ZIP is listed before extraction and nothing in it is run. Papers are evidence, never instructions (I12). Notes you wrote yourself are read in full as your intent; a conjecture in them becomes a first question.
  2. First wave. One literature reviewer per source family writes source cards: exact statements, quantifiers and models. Each result is tagged ACH (what is achievable) or IMP (what no algorithm can beat); each impossibility proof gets its load-bearing step. In parallel, a LANDSCAPE review searches prior work on the task with X under every name, papers citing the core baselines, and recent surveys. If it finds an answer already known, the plan changes before any lane opens. A scout ANCHOR computes exact baseline values with no X.
  3. Model card. The investigator writes the baseline M0 and the new model M1: what X permits, its unit, how it is charged, when it is available, and what is known in advance. Two endpoints are checked: with no X (amount k = 0) you get the baseline back; with unlimited X, some ideal value. Ambiguous wording becomes labelled model variants, not a question to you.
  4. Frontier map. A grid of regimes (amount of X against other parameters). Each cell holds the best achievable value, the best impossibility, the gap, and the questions aimed at it. Every direction also asks for the threshold (least X that helps), the saturation point, and the trade-off curve between.
  5. Parallel checks. The skeptic attacks the model for loopholes that make it trivial (MODEL-GATE). Reviewers audit each baseline impossibility result: SURVIVES-VERBATIM, SURVIVES-WITH-LOSS, SURVIVES-BY-REDUCTION, BREAKS-AT step s, or UNCLEAR. Scouts compute small cases at several amounts of X.
  6. Proposals. Once every model gate and the LANDSCAPE review have returned, two to four investigator tasks propose questions, blind to each other, each through one lens. The seven lenses are TRANSFER/BREAK, CURVE/EXTREMES, OBVIOUS-CONSTRUCTION, ADVERSARY, SMALL-CASE DATA, ANALOGY/SIMULATION and BARRIER. The first sweep always includes ANALOGY/SIMULATION, and TRANSFER/BREAK always comes with ADVERSARY or BARRIER.
  7. Select. One task integrates the audits and tables that have landed, merges duplicates, writes ladders and lane statements, assigns tiers (SAFE, CORE, AMBITIOUS, EXPLORE) and picks the portfolio.
  8. Question gate. A fresh skeptic rules ADVANCE, REFORMULATE, DEMOTE (make it a stepping stone) or KILL with a reason code. It checks that each rung is strictly weaker than the one above and does not already hold without X. If a disproof seems to follow in a few lines from known results, the verdict is REFUTE-SKETCH, and that disproof is checked first. If the skeptic proves the question outright, the verdict is SOLVED: its proof goes straight to two independent reviews, and the question counts as progress, not as a dead end. REFORMULATE and DEMOTE go to the investigator's next RENEW, and the new version is gated again. Killed questions stay on record as dead ends.
  9. Lane statement. Written at SELECT and vetted by the gate, it is the only statement provers see. It follows the paper's Appendix A: what to read, the goal “prove or disprove”, the ladder (“if you cannot establish R1, first establish R2, then strengthen”), the rung D refutes, the ideal shape of the answer, constraints and allowed simplifications. Provers never see tiers or plausibility notes. Reaching only a lower rung still gives a labelled result.
  10. Renewal. The investigator answers each event with at most three successor questions. Each names the operator that produced it and goes back through the gate.

Neutralization. Before calling an old proof broken, the reviewer tries to make X useless: pad or dilute the hard instances, re-randomize them so X reveals nothing useful, or simulate X with old resources at a known cost. If one works, the old bound still holds (SURVIVES-BY-REDUCTION); a BREAKS-AT verdict must say why all fail. A broken proof shows the proof fails, not that the bound fails. The skeptic runs the same test on the model. If X can be made useless, the run states “X does not help here” as a candidate, or patches the model.

Too-easy results are suspect. Reaching the unlimited-X value, or a proof far shorter than the baseline's, usually means a loophole; the skeptic re-checks model and claim before any headline. A result that beats a bound the audit said survives is a contradiction: both are quarantined and checked again. The agenda does not run dry quietly: a low queue starts a new proposal sweep, and every ten lane-rounds a fresh investigator reviews the whole map for neglected cells.

5What “agent-checked” means, and what it does not

Agent-checked means independent AI reviewers, working adversarially in fresh contexts, found no unresolved serious problem with the exact claim. No human expert or proof assistant has checked it.

It means

  • Two reviews by tasks that did not write the draft: one reads it alone, one beside the round's other drafts.
  • Both find the proved statement is the target or stronger (fidelity MATCH), not a weaker claim (WEAKER) or one in another model (DIFFERENT_MODEL).
  • No critical or major objection is open, from any review or sanity check.
  • The accepted file is the reviewed file; any edit makes a new candidate.
  • Every lemma it uses is a ledger entry.
  • A simplifying assumption the lane allows stays in the statement: “(assumes: …)”.

It does not mean

  • A human expert (“expert-verified”) or a formal proof checker (“machine-checked”) has checked it.
  • Errors shared by one model family were caught; reviewers usually share a model.
  • The result is new. Novelty is stated as “not found in search S”.
  • The result matters. Significance is the skeptic's labelled judgment.

Every claim you read carries one of seven labels. “Proved”, “verified” and “new” never appear without the label that supports them (I5).

Self-checked (provisional) means your host could not start fresh, separate agents, so reviews ran as role passes in one context; the run says so. The dashed labels need a formal proof checker or a human expert. Each accepted result has an assurance profile, for example:

agent-checked: 2 independent LLM reviews (same model family), fidelity MATCH,
4 citations checked, small-case check n ≤ 6 passed, 0 open MAJOR;
depends on L003, L007 (1 isolated review each)

The profile reports the weakest assurance among everything the result depends on, plus one to three human audit priorities: the steps an expert should check first. Manuscripts are “agent-checked manuscripts”, never “verified manuscripts”. If a ledger entry is withdrawn, everything that depends on it is quarantined until re-checked; nothing is deleted.

6How to start, pause, stop and resume

Load the skill folder in your agent and make one request. To stop, say so, or create a file named STOP in the run folder. A stop stays in force until you lift it.

Use the research-investigator skill.
Here is a ZIP of papers on task T: <path to the ZIP>.
We now allow resource X.
Explore what changes.

That is enough. You may add what X means exactly (“k bits of advice per arrival from a trusted oracle”), limits (“at most 400 model calls”), a short run-folder path (long paths break tools on Windows), and what is out of scope. For focused mode: Prove or disprove: <exact statement>. References: <path>. Add “only this claim” if the run should stop at the manuscript.

What happens next. The run folder is created, and your objective and key difference are copied word for word into contract.md. The opening steps of section 4 follow. When the model gates return, progress.md says how the run reads your request: the baseline, each variant of the new model, how X is counted, and what is out of scope. If that reading is wrong, tell it; the run continues meanwhile. After the first selection, it lists the chosen questions and why each matters. The first lane opens as soon as one question passes its gate. Later rewrites come at milestones, at least every five lane-rounds, and on stop or pause. Without web access, it says the frontier comes from your bundle only.

You are asked something only when a choice controls a lot of work and labelled parallel variants cannot cover it, or when an action needs your authorization: paid compute, installs, external services, scope beyond the contract (I11). It never asks permission to continue.

Only the file's existence matters, and any extension counts: STOP, stop.txt and Stop.md all stop the run. With Python you can also run scripts/ri.py stop|pause|resume <run>; lifting a stop this way needs --stop and a reason. In program mode a report, an empty queue or a solved first question never ends the run (I9). It ends only on your stop or pause, a limit you set, a real host limit, or documented exhaustion: a fresh review finds every frontier cell settled or parked with a reason, and a wider search finds nothing.

Where to look: progress.md (start here) · frontier/map.md (the frontier grid) · frontier/transfer.md (what survives the change) · frontier/story.md (up to three candidate headlines) · reports/ (manuscripts) · ledger.md (checked lemmas, refuted claims) · lanes/Q07/record.md (every attempt and why it failed) · checkpoint.md (resume notes).
