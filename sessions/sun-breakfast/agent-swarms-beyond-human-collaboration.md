# Agent swarms that do what human collaborations cannot

An idea from Sunday breakfast. **How this note was made:** Will Zeng relayed it and Claude wrote it up. Will did not say who else was in the conversation, so the points are not attributed to individuals. If you were there, add your name under Notes.

## What was discussed

Most multi-agent research setups copy how humans do research: roles, meetings, messages, review. The idea is to stop anthropomorphising and design swarms that do things orthogonal to what humans can do, by building on capabilities that agents have and humans lack.

Examples of such capabilities:

- **Exact cloning.** An agent can be copied exactly, including everything it knows at that moment.
- **Exact memory erasure.** Specific memories can be removed, which no human collaborator can do.
- **Communicating in "neuralese".** Agents could exchange weights or neuron activations directly, instead of tokens.
- **Observers with access to the inside.** An observer could do more than read a sub-agent's output tokens. It could monitor the sub-agent's weights as it performs a task and updates itself, or modify those weights. This was described as, in effect, mind control.
